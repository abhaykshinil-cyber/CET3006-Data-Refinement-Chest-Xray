RESEARCH POSITIONING
--------------------------------------------------
This study is situated within Data-Centric AI, a paradigm that improves model performance by refining training data rather than modifying model architecture. It investigates how Uncertainty Quantification can identify and correct data quality issues in medical imaging datasets, addressing the core problem of Data Quality Optimization: distinguishing harmful data points from beneficial ones under noisy labelling conditions.

RESEARCH QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------
  RQ1: How can uncertainty estimation identify low-quality or mislabelled samples in medical datasets?
  RQ2: What is the optimal level of data cleaning that improves model performance without discarding useful information?
  RQ3: How does selective data refinement compare to training on noisy datasets in terms of generalisation performance?

ABSTRACT
--------------------------------------------------
We present a data-centric pipeline for chest X-ray pneumonia classification in which model improvement is achieved by refining training data rather than modifying architecture. A ResNet18 backbone pretrained on ImageNet is fine-tuned on a binary NORMAL/PNEUMONIA dataset under controlled label noise (20% symmetric flip). Monte Carlo Dropout (T=30 stochastic forward passes) estimates predictive uncertainty per sample; flagged samples are ranked by uncertainty and removed at thresholds of 10%, 20%, and 30% of the flagged pool. The noisy baseline achieves F1=93.31%. Selective 10% cleaning yields the best result (F1=94.51%, delta=+1.21pp), while aggressive cleaning degrades performance, demonstrating that data quality optimisation requires preserving informative samples rather than maximising removals.

DETECTION RESULTS  (RQ1)
--------------------------------------------------
MC Dropout flagged 663 of 4,099 training samples (16.2%) as suspicious using two complementary criteria:
  Criterion A: predictive variance > threshold (high epistemic uncertainty)
  Criterion B: confident mismatch (model predicts wrong class with high confidence)
Against the known noise ground-truth: precision=94.4%,  recall=76.3%.
Uncertainty thus serves as a proxy for label quality, directly addressing RQ1.

KEY FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------
  [FINDING 1 - Selective Cleaning Works]
  CLEANED_10pct removes only the most uncertain flagged samples (10pct of the flagged pool) and achieves F1=94.51% (+1.21pp vs noisy). This directly answers RQ2: a conservative threshold maximises the benefit of cleaning.

  [FINDING 2 - Aggressive Cleaning Fails]
  CLEANED_20pct achieves F1=92.68% (-0.63pp vs noisy), confirming that beyond the optimal threshold, removal discards samples that carry genuine learning signal, reducing robustness and diversity.

  [FINDING 3 - Confidence-Weighted Loss as Soft Alternative]
  WEIGHTED training (no sample removal, weight_floor=0.2) achieves F1=94.12% (+0.81pp vs noisy), demonstrating that downweighting rather than discarding suspicious samples is a viable strategy that preserves dataset size.

CORE INSIGHT
--------------------------------------------------
  'Improving data quality is not equivalent to reducing dataset size.'
  Data quality optimisation requires selectively preserving informative samples while removing harmful noise. High-uncertainty samples are not uniformly detrimental; some carry rare but valid patterns essential for generalisation. Excessive filtering degrades performance by reducing diversity.

MAIN CONTRIBUTION
--------------------------------------------------
  This work demonstrates that selective, uncertainty-guided data refinement can effectively mitigate label noise while preserving informative samples, leading to improved model performance in medical imaging classification. The pipeline is fully reproducible, requires no manual relabelling, and is applicable to any dataset where annotation noise is a concern.

REAL-WORLD RELEVANCE
--------------------------------------------------
  In real-world healthcare systems, datasets are inherently noisy due to annotation variability and distributed data collection across institutions. A data-centric approach enables scalable dataset improvement without costly manual relabelling -- particularly relevant for systems such as the NHS, where data originates from multiple hospitals with inconsistent labelling standards.
